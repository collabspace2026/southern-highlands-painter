Preparing Your Home for a Repaint — A Southern Highlands Checklist

By Dan Eaton • August 21, 2026 • 9 min read

Once you’ve booked in a repaint, there’s a bit of groundwork that makes the whole process smoother — for you, for your painters, and for the end result. Whether it’s an interior refresh or a full exterior repaint, a little preparation on your end goes a long way toward a job that runs on schedule and finishes to a high standard.

Here’s the checklist we generally walk clients through before starting a job across the Southern Highlands.

Before the Quote: Know What You Want

It helps to have a rough idea of scope before your quote appointment — are you after a full exterior repaint, just the trims and window frames, an interior refresh of specific rooms, or everything? If you already have colour ideas or inspiration, bring them along; if you don’t, a good colour consultation as part of the quote process can help you land on something that suits your home.

It’s also worth mentioning anything unusual about the property upfront — heritage overlay restrictions, known issues like water damage or previous paint failures, or areas that are difficult to access — so the quote reflects the real scope of the job.

For Exterior Jobs

  • Trim back any garden growth that’s in contact with the walls, fences or eaves — this needs to be clear for both preparation and painting
  • Move outdoor furniture, pot plants, barbecues and other items away from the areas being painted
  • Take down anything mounted on the walls that could be affected — house numbers, lights, hanging baskets — if they’re easily removable
  • Let your painter know about any garden beds or landscaping that need extra protection from drop sheets or overspray
  • If you have pets, think through how they’ll be managed around the property while gates may be open and equipment is in use
  • Check with neighbours if scaffolding or access equipment might need to go on or over a shared boundary

For Interior Jobs

  • Clear the rooms being painted as much as possible — furniture, wall art, curtains and rugs
  • For furniture too large to move out of the room, pull it into the centre so it can be covered and worked around
  • Remove items from walls — picture hooks, shelving, mirrors — and patch any holes you want filled if that’s not part of the painter’s scope
  • Take down curtains and blinds, or let your painter know if you’d like them removed as part of the job
  • Plan around the practical disruption — if it’s a kitchen or bathroom, think about how you’ll manage while it’s out of use
  • Let your painter know about any surfaces with existing damage — cracks, water stains, or areas that have been patched before — so they can be properly addressed

Talk Through Access and Timing

If you won’t be home for all or part of the job, work out access arrangements in advance — a key, a code, or a time you’ll be available to let painters in. It’s also worth discussing expected timing upfront, including how weather might affect an exterior job, so there are no surprises if a day or two needs to shift around conditions.

Understand What’s Included

Before work starts, it’s worth being clear on exactly what’s covered in your quote — number of coats, whether minor repairs and gap filling are included, what happens if additional preparation is needed once old paint is stripped back, and what’s not included. A good painter will walk you through this rather than leaving it vague, and it saves any awkward conversations partway through the job.

Sorting Out Practical Details in Advance

A few small logistics are easy to overlook until the day work starts. If there’s limited parking or a shared driveway, let your painter know so vehicles and equipment can be arranged without causing issues for neighbours. If your property has dogs, make sure gates that need to stay open during the job won’t create a safety problem. And if you’re having exterior and interior work done together, it’s worth discussing the order of operations with your painter, since some sequencing makes the overall job more efficient than others.

During the Job

Once work is underway, a few things help keep things running smoothly:

  1. Keep pets and small children away from work areas, particularly where drop sheets, ladders or open paint tins are in use
  2. Avoid touching freshly painted surfaces, even if they look dry — paint can take longer to fully cure than it takes to dry to the touch
  3. Let your painter know straight away if you notice anything you’re not happy with, rather than waiting until the job is finished
  4. Keep windows and doors in the work area accessible for ventilation, particularly for interior jobs

After the Job

Once painting is complete, give the paint proper time to cure fully before hanging anything back on the walls or moving furniture back into position — your painter can advise on timing, which varies by product and conditions. For exteriors, it’s worth doing a walk-around with your painter before they finish up, so any final touch-ups can be addressed while they’re still on site.

Questions Worth Asking Your Painter

Before work begins, a short conversation can save confusion later. Useful questions to ask include: how many coats are included, what preparation work is covered versus additional repairs that might come up once old paint is stripped back, what happens if the weather delays the job, what paint brands and products will be used, and whether there’s any warranty or guarantee on the workmanship. A painter who answers these clearly and directly, without hedging, is generally a good sign.

Choosing the Right Time of Year

If your schedule allows some flexibility, spring and autumn are generally the most reliable seasons for exterior work in the Southern Highlands, with a wider daily window of suitable painting conditions than the depths of winter. If your job needs to happen in winter or the middle of summer, that’s still entirely workable — it just means your painter needs to plan more carefully around the weather each day, which is worth discussing upfront so you know what to expect in terms of timing.

A Little Preparation Goes a Long Way

None of this is complicated, but taking care of it before the job starts means less time lost on-site clearing space, and a smoother process overall. If you’re not sure what applies to your specific job, just ask — we’re happy to run through exactly what to expect and what to prepare when we come out for your free quote.

We’ve walked hundreds of Southern Highlands homeowners through this process over the years, and the jobs that run most smoothly are almost always the ones where a bit of preparation happened on both sides before the first brush went near a wall. It’s a small investment of time that makes the whole experience easier, start to finish.
